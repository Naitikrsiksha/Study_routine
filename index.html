01 · Know the machine you are fighting

Half your marks are circles on an OMR sheet.

This is the single most useful fact in your whole preparation. BSEB sets far more questions than you must answer, so the paper rewards recognition and speed, not long essays. Build your plan around that.

Physics · Chemistry · Biology70 + 30

70 theory + 30 practical. 42 MCQs are set, you answer any 35 (35 marks). Then 10 of 18 two-markers (20 marks), and 3 of 6 five-markers (15 marks).

Hindi · English100

100-mark theory paper. 60 MCQs set, answer any 50. Short answers and long answers make up the rest, with writing skills in English.

Choice in the paper1 in 6

You get 42 MCQs for 35 answers. Mark the ones you are certain of first, then use the spare choice to skip the traps. Never leave a bubble empty.

Wrong answersFree

No negative marking anywhere. In the objective section, guessing costs nothing — an unmarked bubble is the only guaranteed zero.

Passing33%

Physics, Chemistry, Biology: 21/70 theory + 12/30 practical. Hindi and English: 30/100. Clear the practical comfortably and the theory target drops.

Time on the desk3h 15m

15 minutes are reading time — use them to pick your 35 MCQs mentally before the bell. Budget 40 minutes for objective, 90 for two-markers, 45 for long answers.

Three things almost everyone gets wrong

  • Biology is not a Reproduction subject. Reproduction carries just 6 of 70 marks, while Genetics & Evolution, Biology & Human Welfare, and Biotechnology carry 18 each — 54 marks between them. Most students spend October on flowering plants and panic in January.
  • Your NCERT book is missing chapters that BSEB will examine. Physics Communication Systems (7 marks), and Chemistry's Solid State, Surface Chemistry, Isolation of Elements, Polymers and Chemistry in Everyday Life, are in the Bihar Board syllabus but were dropped from the new NCERT edition. Study these from the Bihar Board textbook, not NCERT.
  • Hindi and English are not "easy filler" subjects. They are 200 of your 500 marks and the fastest to improve, because MCQ literature and grammar reward recall, not reasoning. Two hours a day for three weeks moves these two subjects more than any science chapter will.

04 · The daily engine

What a day actually looks like.

Hardest work goes in the first slot, when your mind is fresh. Memory work goes late, because recall before sleep sticks. The last ten minutes are non-negotiable: that is where the error notebook gets written.

  • 8:00–8:30Error notebook + next day's plan

06 · How to not forget it

Reading once is not studying.

You will forget 70% of a chapter within a week unless you meet it again. These five habits are what separate a 60-day plan that works from one that just looks organised.

The 1–3–7–21 rule

Every chapter gets revisited on day 1, day 3, day 7 and day 21 after first reading. Each revisit is shorter than the last — 30 minutes, then 15, then 5.

The error notebook

One notebook, one page per subject per week. Only mistakes go in it — the question, why you got it wrong, and the correct one-liner. Revise this, not the textbook, in the last month.

35 MCQs a day

Half the paper is objective. Do one 35-question set daily from the chapters you have finished. It is the only practice that directly matches how marks are actually scored.

Two sheets per subject

A formula sheet (Physics, Chemistry numericals) and a diagram sheet (Biology, Ray Optics, circuits). By December these two sheets should be able to rebuild the whole subject.

Answer like an examiner

Two-mark answers: two lines, definition plus example. Five-mark answers: a heading, four numbered points, one diagram or equation. Never write a paragraph when a list will do.

Write, don't highlight

Reading feels productive and is the weakest form of study. After every chapter, close the book and write the map from memory on blank paper. What you cannot write, you do not know.
